A pseudo-brand designed to unify Emily Astranova's creations

Brand guide

AstraLabs is the brand Emily Astranova uses for her homelab and the projects she shares. It goes on dashboards, login screens, READMEs, SSH banners, stickers and video intros. The look is a clean lab with a little hangar grit: precise line work and technical labels, Acid green on carbon black, and imperfection that arrives as brief events (a flicker, a glitch) rather than as texture.

Content fundamentals

  • Voice: dry, technical, deadpan. Write like an equipment label or a man page, with the occasional straight-faced joke. "Handle with intent." "Property of AstraLabs." "Mounting /astra/labs."
  • Real information only. Every label, serial and barcode encodes something true: a hostname, a port, a version, a build hash. Never use filler like "Lorem" or "SYS-000". Good: node-02.astralabs.dev, PORT 8443 / TLS, AL-0451-NODE02 REV C.
  • Casing: mono labels are UPPERCASE (label style). Headlines in headline-xl and title are uppercase. Body copy and buttons' sentence text are sentence case. Hostnames and paths keep their real lowercase.
  • Person: address the reader as "you" sparingly. Status text is terse and impersonal: "Mounting /astra/labs", "Reconnected", "Survey complete".
  • No emoji. Use the geometric glyphs ■ ▲ ● and plain words.
  • Buttons say exactly what happens: "Authenticate", "Restart service", "Wipe pool". The result echoes it: "Restarted".

Logo

  • The mark is always white (mark). Never recolour it, split it into colours, outline it on a light ground, or put it on acid. On the rare light surface, use the carbon file. Files are in the Logos asset group.
  • Geometry: an exact equilateral triangle built from two ribbons. Stroke 66 and gap 33 on a 600-unit width, so gap = stroke ÷ 2 everywhere. Never redraw it. Use the files or the Mark component.
  • Wordmark: Bracketed Path. [ASTRA/LABS] in Martian Mono 300, width 112.5, tracking 0.36em, uppercase (wordmark style). Letters are mark white. Brackets and slash are acid. Always use the Wordmark or Lockup component, or the outlined lockup SVGs. Never retype it in another face.
  • Lockups: horizontal for headers and footers, stacked for avatars and splash screens. In the horizontal lockup the wordmark is 0.56× the mark's height unit, and the gap is 0.42×.
  • Clear space: one stroke width (11% of the mark's width) on every side.
  • Minimum sizes: mark 16px tall. Horizontal lockup 160px wide. Below that, use the mark alone. The single-ink lockup (astralabs-lockup-mono.svg) is for one-colour contexts like engraving, terminals without colour, and favicons in monochrome themes.

Colour

  • Build on void. Raise surfaces with plate and divide them with rule hairlines. No drop shadows.
  • acid is the only brand colour. It marks brand moments and interaction: primary buttons, the wordmark's brackets and slash, hazard stripes, decal bars, focus. Keep it under about 10% of any screen.
  • bone is the accent for line work and live data: construction lines, callouts, readout values, sparklines, outlines. It is also the body text colour.
  • ash is for labels and secondary text only. Never use it for anything you need to read quickly.
  • Status (status-ok, status-warn, status-down) is for dashboards only and is rarely used. Always pair each colour with its shape (solid square, striped square, blinking square, ring for sync) and a word. status-ok sits close to acid, so it never appears outside a status pill.
  • Text on acid fills uses on-acid.

Typography

  • Archivo (display, Google Fonts) carries headlines and body copy. Set headline-xl and title uppercase, using width 110–118 through font-variation-settings.
  • Martian Mono (mono, shipped as a variable font) carries everything technical: label (width 85, uppercase), data, readout and the wordmark. Use font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums wherever digits line up.
  • Keep running text near 65 characters. Never set body copy in mono.

Geometry and layout

  • One angle: 60° (angle-cut). Hazard stripes, chamfered corners and the slash all lean at the mark's angle.
  • Square corners (radius-none) everywhere. The 60° cut is rare and means something: one cut per view. Only the filled primary button (chamfer-sm) and the destructive dialog (chamfer-md) are cut, always top-right. Outlined elements are never cut. radius-dot is only for rings and hubs.
  • Nothing tilts. Decals, tags and panels sit square to the grid.
  • 16px lab grid (space-4). Lay sheets out as hairline-ruled cells: shared borders, no gaps, no cards floating on shadows.
  • Page gutters: space-4 on phones, up to space-12 on wide screens. Sections sit space-18 apart.

Decals and line work

  • Hazard band: acid stripes at 60°. Use only for maintenance mode, destructive actions, and the foot of asset tags. Never as decoration.
  • Barcode + serial: encode a real ID. One per view.
  • Registration marks: acid crosshairs in frame corners. Two per view at most.
  • Focus brackets: acid corner brackets that snap in around the selected or focused thing.
  • Callouts and dimensions: bone hairlines with data labels for annotating diagrams and network maps.
  • Gauge: one 2px rule ring, one 2px acid arc for the value, four ash quarter markers outside the ring, and the number in the centre. Nothing else.

Line markers and dot grids

Detail comes from measurement, not decoration. Each marker shows a real boundary, value or relationship.

  • Measurement rule: a rule hairline with 9px ash end ticks and a 32px acid index segment at its start. It sits under every section head and above footers.
  • Corner ticks: four 8px L-marks in bone at 55% instead of a full frame. Use them on stat panels, toasts, the command palette and stages. Acid ticks mean selected.
  • Leaders: dotted ash lines between a key and its value in spec lists and menus.
  • Thresholds and scales: meters get a bone threshold marker with a small triangle, and a quarter-step scale underneath. Sliders get a ruler with 8px minor and 40px major ticks.
  • Dot grids: 1px dots at 22% bone, 16px apart. Keep them flat and faint behind heroes, empty states, sparklines and drop zones. No radial glows. Use 8px spacing for small charts.

Graphs

  • One series: draw it in acid. Two or more: chart-1, chart-2, chart-3 in that order, always, with chart-other for the rest. Colour follows the entity, never its rank. These steps passed the lightness, chroma, colour-blind and contrast checks on void; brand acid and bone fail them as a series pair (both too light, bone reads as grey).
  • Marks: 2px lines with round joins, a 10% wash under a single line, bars at most 24px thick with a 4px rounded data end and a square baseline, end markers 8px with a 2px void ring, 2px void gaps between stacked segments.
  • Chrome: solid rule-subtle gridlines and a rule baseline, never dashed. A dashed bone line means a threshold. Tick labels in ash mono with tabular figures.
  • Labels: text never takes the series colour. Label the end value, the peak or a threshold, never every point. Two or more series always get a legend, and each series is also labelled at its end.
  • Every chart reads as a table (a Table toggle), and hover shows a crosshair and tooltip on lines and per-mark tooltips on bars.
  • Never two y-axes on one chart.

States

  • Focus: a 2px solid acid outline at 3px offset (focus stroke). That gives 16:1 on void and 15:1 on plate.
  • Hover: primary buttons brighten to acid-hi. Ghost buttons fill with acid. Quiet buttons take a bone border. Colour changes are hard cuts (Flick). The Lockup's brackets push outward.
  • Disabled: 40% opacity, no hover.
  • Destructive: status-down text on a ghost button, with a hazard band nearby.

Iconography

AstraLabs has no icon library yet. Use the geometric glyphs ■ ▲ ●, short mono words, and line-work drawings in bone. If an icon set is needed later, pick a 1.5px-stroke outline set with square caps and set its colour to bone. Flagged: no icon set is defined in this system.

Motion

See the Motion section. In short: four speeds (Flick, Snap, Trace, Settle), three patterns (Strobe-in, Wipe-in, Stagger), and grit as events. Glitches and flickers run under 400ms, one at a time, with an idle glitch no more often than every 8–20 seconds. Respect prefers-reduced-motion by showing the finished state.

Logos

AstraLabs logo files. All are flat vector SVGs with the wordmark text converted to outlines, so they need no fonts.

  • astralabs-mark.svg: the mark, white #FFFFFF. The primary logo. Use on void and plate.
  • astralabs-mark-carbon.svg: the mark in carbon #0A0909. Only for the rare light surface (paper, a light OS theme).
  • astralabs-lockup.svg: horizontal lockup. Mark and letters in white, brackets and slash in acid #B6FF2E. Headers, footers, README banners.
  • astralabs-lockup-mono.svg: horizontal lockup in single-ink white. For one-colour contexts.
  • astralabs-lockup-stacked.svg: stacked lockup, white and acid. Avatars, splash screens, title cards.
  • astralabs-square.svg: square icon. Carbon ground, 32-unit lab grid in rule, white mark optically centred inside the circular-crop safe zone. Discord servers, Matrix rooms, app icons.
  • astralabs-square-reg.svg: the square icon plus two acid registration marks on grid intersections. Only where the square is shown uncropped (GitHub orgs, Gitea, favicons on square tiles). Circular crops cut the marks off.

Keep one stroke width (11% of the mark's width) of clear space on all sides. Never recolour, stretch or redraw. Mark minimum is 16px tall, horizontal lockup minimum is 160px wide.

Components

Live previews rendered with the component bundle (window.AstraLabs, React 18).

Mark

Brand

The AstraLabs mark: two ribbons forming an equilateral A, always in mark white.

Provide: size (height in px, default 32, minimum 16). Pass title only when the mark stands alone, so screen readers get a name. Pass trace to play the boot trace.

Use as the favicon, the avatar, and in the corner of a page when the full lockup will not fit.

  • Do keep it white on void or plate.
  • Do give it one stroke width of clear space.
  • Don't recolour it, split it into colours, put it on acid, rotate it, or redraw it.

Wordmark

Brand

The Bracketed Path wordmark, [ASTRA/LABS]: Martian Mono 300 with white letters and an acid slash and brackets.

Provide: size (letter size in px). For the boot sequence pass boot: it strobes on as [ / ] with a spinning slash, ASTRA types in left of the spinner, LABS on its right, then the slash settles and the brackets snap. Change bootKey to replay it. For a loading state pass loading: the slash spins while the brackets stay solid, then the brackets snap when it turns false.

Use on its own only in system moments: terminal banners, loading screens, file paths. Everywhere else use Lockup.

  • Do use the component or the outlined SVGs. The spacing is exact.
  • Don't retype it in another font, drop the brackets, or recolour the letters.
  • Don't leave loading on for decoration. It means something is loading.

Lockup

Brand

The mark and the Bracketed Path wordmark together. This is the primary logo.

Provide: size (height unit in px, default 40) and variant (horizontal or stacked). Optional boot plays the full sequence: the mark traces, then the path types. interactive (default true) pushes the brackets outward on hover.

Use horizontal in headers, footers and README banners. Use stacked for avatars, splash screens and video title cards.

  • Do keep it on void or plate, with one stroke width of clear space.
  • Do use the mark alone below 160px wide.
  • Don't rearrange the parts or change the size ratio between them.

Button

Actions

A square button. Only the filled primary button carries the 60° cut, so the cut marks the one main action in a view.

Provide: children (the action, in a few words) and variant: primary (acid fill with the cut, one per view), ghost (acid outline), quiet (rule outline, for secondary actions) or danger (status-down outline). Takes all native button attributes.

  • Do name the exact action: "Authenticate", "Restart service".
  • Do pair danger with a confirm step and, for big actions, a HazardBand.
  • Don't use more than one primary button in a view. Never cut an outlined button.

FocusFrame

Actions

Acid corner brackets that snap in around their content to show focus or selection.

Provide: children and active (default true). Toggle active to replay the snap.

  • Do use it for the selected item in a grid, or a highlighted target.
  • Don't leave it on several things at once.

Panel

Data

A square plate-coloured container marked by four corner ticks instead of a full frame.

Provide: label and value (with optional unit) for a stat, or children for anything else. active turns the corner ticks acid for the selected panel.

  • Do lay panels on the 16px grid with space-3 gaps.
  • Don't nest panels, add shadows, or cut their corners.

Readout

Data

A label over a live value in mono: the smallest unit of data.

Provide: label (uppercase, short) and value, with an optional unit.

  • Do use real values with tabular numbers.
  • Don't use it for prose.

StatusPill

Data

A service status shown as a shape, a colour and a word.

Provide: state: ok (solid square), warn (striped square), down (blinking square) or sync (ring). Pass children to override the default word.

Use in dashboards and service lists only. Status colours never appear elsewhere in the brand.

  • Do keep the word. Colour alone never carries status.

Dial

Data

A gauge for one value: a thin ring, an acid arc, four quarter markers and the number in the centre.

Provide: value (0 to 1), an optional label (centre text, default is the percentage) and sublabel (what it measures, such as "CPU"). size in px, default 112.

  • Do use it for single values: load, memory, storage.
  • Do keep it plain. No extra ticks, segments or glows.
  • Don't use more than three in a row; use a table or meters instead.

HazardBand

Decals

Acid stripes at the mark's 60° angle.

Provide: height in px (default 16). It fills the width of its container.

Use only for maintenance mode, destructive actions, and the foot of asset tags.

  • Don't use it as a divider or decoration.

SerialTag

Decals

A barcode over a serial line. The barcode is generated from the serial, so the same ID always draws the same bars.

Provide: serial (a real ID: hostname, version, build hash) and an optional rev.

  • Do keep one per view.
  • Don't invent serials.

AssetTag

Decals

A property-tag decal: an acid header bar, the lockup, then a hazard band and barcode foot.

Provide: id (the asset number) and an optional header (default "Property of").

Use for stickers on hardware, README footers and the login screens of self-hosted services.

Terminal

Surfaces

An SSH login banner (/etc/motd) with the lockup, status lines and a prompt.

Provide: host, user, an optional lastLogin, and lines (strings or nodes, short, real stats).

Use for the real motd on your machines and for terminal screenshots in docs.

Motion

AstraLabs moves like lab equipment powering up. Precise and fast, with brief mechanical imperfections. Motion values live in components/bundle.css as --al-t- (durations) and --al-e- (easings), because the token file has no motion family.

Speeds

TokenDurationEasingUse for
Flick--al-t-flick 40mssteps(1, end)Single frames: glitch slices, tube flicker, blinking status squares
Snap--al-t-snap 200mscubic-bezier(.3, 1.35, .5, 1)A servo: small overshoot, settles. Brackets, toggles, sliding indicators, focus
Trace--al-t-trace 900mscubic-bezier(.7, 0, .2, 1)Winds up, commits, lands precisely. Lines drawing on, meters, scans, wipes across a frame
Settle--al-t-settle 360mscubic-bezier(.16, 1, .3, 1)Arrives at once, decelerates long. Anything people read: text, panels, toasts

Patterns

  • Strobe-in (340ms): opacity off, on, off, on, dim (35%), on, in hard steps, then holds. Only for things that power on: the mark, wordmark brackets and slash, tick marks, registration marks. Never on rows, panels or running text.
  • Trace-in (600–1000ms, Trace): a box draws its own border. A 1px bone line runs clockwise from the top-left (sheet cells draw their right edge down, then their bottom edge left; the sheet draws its left edge up, then its top edge right), then cools to rule. For boxes in video, boot screens and presentation pages.
  • Light-up (900ms): contents go from unlit (10% opacity, dimmed) to lit. One small flick to 60% at 16–28%, then a rise with a soft acid glow (drop-shadow 6px at 35%) that fades out. Starts about 260ms after the box's trace, for what's inside it.
  • Wipe-in (560ms, Settle): clip-path: inset(0 100% 0 0) → inset(0). Smooth, with no flicker. Only for lines drawing on, such as measurement rules.
  • Stagger (60ms per item): siblings in lists and grids enter one after another. Each item is its own visible beat; the list still reads as one motion.
  • Interfaces don't animate in. Dashboards, service pages and other working UI are simply there when they load, ready to read, with no traces, light-ups, wipes or count-ups. Motion in UI responds to what the user does: hover, toggles, spinners, toasts, the deploy pipeline. The one exception is the login boot.
  • Trace-in, Light-up, Wipe-in and Stagger are for video, title cards, boot screens and presentation pages, where an entrance is the point.

Grit is an event

  • In script-driven animation (WAAPI), put steps(1, end) on each keyframe, never on the whole animation. Applied to the whole animation it holds the first frame for the full duration and then jumps to the end.
  • Glitches, flickers and signal drops happen on a state change, then stop. No looping scanlines, no CRT texture, no noise overlays.
  • Keep every grit effect under 400ms, except flicker-on (700ms) and signal drop (900ms). Run one at a time.
  • An idle glitch on a resting mark may fire at random every 8–20 seconds, never more often, and never while the tab is hidden.
  • The glitch is a burst of 7–13 stepped frames (26–62ms each) whose strength rises and falls, then snaps back to a clean frame. Each frame mixes five layers: chromatic split (true red, green and blue channel copies drift 1–3px apart, red right and blue left, under a 45% pass of the clean image, so fringes stay faint and brand colours rebuild exactly), line shift (rows 1–7px tall; one band slides hard, scattered rows slip), block tear (up to four rectangles displaced sideways, sometimes leaving an acid sliver), frame jitter (1–3px jumps) and dropout (a frame at 15–50% brightness). Intensity runs Subtle 0.35, Default 0.6, Heavy 1.0.

Signature sequences

  • Boot (Wordmark, Lockup boot): the mark traces its outline in bone (Trace), then its fill flickers on like a fluorescent tube, with two false starts and then a hold. The wordmark then strobes on as just [ / ] with the slash spinning (/ — \ |, 90ms). After about 480ms, ASTRA types in to the left of the spinner at 60ms per letter. After a 200ms beat, LABS types in to its right. The slash settles to / and the brackets snap outward and back (Snap).
  • Loading (Wordmark loading): the slash becomes a spinner cycling / — \ | every 90ms while the brackets stay solid. When done, the slash settles to / and the brackets snap.
  • Focus (FocusFrame, Lockup hover): corner brackets snap in from 2× scale. On hover, the wordmark's brackets push outward 0.45em.
  • Decode: characters resolve left to right through the glyph set ▲◆/\|<>#01ΛΣΔ. Brackets stay still, and the slash locks in last. Use for titles and hostnames as they load.
  • Flicker-on: panels appear with the tube keyframes (700ms). Use for services coming up.
  • Registration align: crosshairs cut in and out at two or three random spots (70–130ms on, 50–120ms off), then strobe into their final position and hold. Their position never animates between spots.
  • Dot field ignition: the full grid first shows unlit (11% bone) for 450ms. Then a ring expands from the mark's centre over 1.3s on a cubic ease-in-out, lighting its dots acid and leaving them at resting brightness. The slow start means the ring is visibly born at the centre. Use it behind boot screens and the login.
  • Dot field idle (Sea): after ignition the dots never move. Their grey rises and falls by about ±14% around the resting brightness (±6% was too faint to see), following a slow height field like ocean swell seen from above: four long waves (300–760px wavelengths, 6–11px/s, 18°, 112°, 205° and 290°) summed so they interfere. It ramps in over 2s, pauses off screen and in hidden tabs, and is static under reduced motion. Moving the dots themselves was tried and dropped: it caused motion sickness.
  • Signal drop: a live value cuts out for about 600ms and comes back changed. Use for reconnects and refreshes.

Graphs in motion

  • Live scroll: time slides at exactly the sample rate, linearly. The right edge shows one sample ago, so new points slide in rather than pop.
  • Rescale: when the data leaves the scale, the y-axis eases to the next nice maximum on Settle (360ms). Gridlines and tick labels slide; nothing jumps.
  • Column push: the newest column grows from the baseline on Snap (200ms); the rest slide one slot per interval.
  • Threshold event: a crossing strobes the threshold line once in status-warn, drops a marker on the time axis at the interpolated crossing, and turns the end marker warn while the value stays above.
  • Charts that are off screen stop rendering. Under reduced motion they update in steps, with no scroll or easing.

Reduced motion

Under prefers-reduced-motion: reduce, show every component in its finished state. No typing, no spinner (show the static / with the status word), and no glitch. The bundle's components check this themselves.
